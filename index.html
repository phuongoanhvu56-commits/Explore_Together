01 / DISCOVER

A WORLD OF EXTREMES

Mars is one of the most studied planets in our Solar System. But reaching it is only the beginning. Living there would mean adapting to an environment very different from Earth.

🌫️
ATMOSPHERE

Thin Air

Mars has a very thin atmosphere composed mostly of carbon dioxide. Humans cannot breathe it, so a habitat must provide controlled breathable air.

❄️
TEMPERATURE

A Cold Planet

Mars is much colder than Earth. A crewed habitat would need insulation and temperature-control systems.

☢️
RADIATION

Radiation Exposure

Mars lacks Earth's global magnetic field and thick atmosphere, making radiation protection an important mission challenge.

02 / SURVIVAL CHALLENGES

COULD YOU LIVE ON MARS?

A successful Mars habitat would need several systems working together.

💧

Water

Water is essential for drinking, hygiene and growing food. Future habitats may recycle wastewater and investigate accessible local ice.

⚡

Energy

Solar panels can generate electricity on Mars, but dust and changing sunlight make energy storage and backup systems important.

🌱

Food

Plants could be grown in controlled environments with carefully managed light, water, nutrients and temperature.

03 / THE SCIENCE

ENGINEERING A HABITAT

Explore technologies and scientific ideas that could help humans live and work on Mars.

SYSTEM 01

Oxygen Generation

NASA's MOXIE experiment aboard Perseverance demonstrated that oxygen can be produced from Martian atmospheric carbon dioxide using solid oxide electrolysis. A crew-scale system would need to be much larger.

SYSTEM 02

Water Recovery

Water-recovery systems can collect and process wastewater. Recycling reduces the amount transported from Earth; it does not mean every Martian water source is accessible.

SYSTEM 03

Radiation Shielding

Habitat designs may use shielding materials and careful placement to reduce exposure. Martian regolith is one resource researchers study for shielding concepts.

SYSTEM 04

Power and Energy

Solar arrays can provide electrical power, while batteries or other systems can help when sunlight is limited. Dust and environmental conditions matter.

SYSTEM 05

Controlled Agriculture

Growing food would likely require enclosed spaces. Plants need suitable light, water, nutrients, temperature and atmospheric conditions.

SYSTEM 06

Habitat Atmosphere

A crew habitat needs controlled pressure, temperature, humidity and breathable air, supported by monitoring equipment.

Scientific accuracy: These are real research areas and engineering concepts, not a claim that an operational human settlement already exists on Mars. Explore Together uses simplified educational simulations in its game.
